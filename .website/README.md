**One button. Flip gravity. Graze a cloud gate and time slows down.**

Tap, or press Space / Up / Down / W / S, and gravity inverts. That is the whole control scheme.

**The graze.** Through the middle of a gate is safe. Close to an edge is a GRAZE: the world dilates around you while your own control stays sharp, the combo climbs, a second rainbow blooms behind your trail, and the soundtrack drops an octave. Tighter miss, deeper slowdown, bigger score - NEAR, GREAT, PERFECT. Four in ten stars sit out on an edge line, ringed and worth 2.5x, reachable only by grazing for them. The safe line and the greedy line are never the same line.

**Four pickups, none ever wasted.** STAR points, GEM shield, PRISM double score for 8s (they stack), HEART a life back. A gem you cannot use pays points instead.

**The journey.** Five lives, four skies. Aurora and shooting stars arrive later on, and the music gains instruments sky by sky. Reach the end of the rainbow and the same meter becomes a nine-rung rank gauge running all the way to COUNTER STOP at 13,312 - this competition's own byte limit, used as the score cap. It takes about eight minutes of near-perfect flying. Five badges persist between sessions.

**No images, no audio files, no fonts.** Every pixel and every note is generated at runtime, including the rainbow ribbon it draws of your entire flight on the result screen, so no two result cards ever look alike. Fixed 60Hz logic on any monitor, 30Hz to 240Hz.

P or ESC pause and rules, R restart, M sound, C copy your result. The title screen is an attract mode: the game plays itself until you touch it.

I built GRAVITY FLIP-2026 for a very different size limit: 1,474,560 bytes, one 3.5-inch floppy disk, with the floppy itself as the hero. At 13KB the original does not fit - I measured it, roughly 2.5x over even gzipped - so I kept only the ideas I liked and rebuilt it from an empty file.
